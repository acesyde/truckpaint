Export Mod turns your project into one ready-to-install mod: a single `.scs` file that holds the paint job of every vehicle of the fleet, with its textures, its shop icon and its entry in the game's Mod Manager.

## Fill in the mod information

The mod's settings belong to the project. Edit them in the <Ui id="cmd-space-project" /> space (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>1</kbd>, <kbd>⌘</kbd> on macOS), in the <Ui id="project-mod-information" /> column on the right. Each change is saved with the project and can be undone.

<Shot name="export/mod-information" alt="The Project space with the Mod information column: the shop icon, the Mod Manager image and the mod settings" />

- **<Ui id="mod-icon" />** (256 × 64) and **<Ui id="mod-image" />** (276 × 162): generated from your first main texture. To use your own picture, click <Ui id="mod-choose-icon" /> or <Ui id="mod-choose-image" />, or drop a PNG or JPEG file on the preview. <Ui id="mod-generated-icon" /> and <Ui id="mod-generated-image" /> bring the generated picture back.
- **<Ui id="mod-name" />:** the paint job's name in the shop and the mod's name in the Mod Manager. It starts as the project name.
- **<Ui id="mod-author" />** and **<Ui id="mod-version" />** (`1.0` at first), shown in the Mod Manager.
- **<Ui id="project-game-versions" />:** the game versions your mod is made for, written as the game writes them, such as `1.56.*`. Click <Ui id="project-game-versions-add" /> to add one, or several separated by commas. Below the list, TruckPaint shows the versions every vehicle of the project supports.
- **<Ui id="mod-description" />:** shown in the Mod Manager. TruckPaint adds the list of vehicles the mod paints.
- **<Ui id="mod-price" />** (5000 at first) and **<Ui id="mod-unlock" />** (0): what the paint job costs in the game's currency, and the player level that unlocks it.
- **<Ui id="mod-advanced" />** › **<Ui id="mod-internal-name" />:** the paint job's identifier in the game files, made from the Name (only a–z, 0–9 and _). Change it if you want, keeping it unique to you, so that your mods don't collide with others.

## Fix what blocks the export

Under <Ui id="project-before-exporting" />, the column lists what must be fixed first, with <Ui id="project-show" /> to jump to the right field or vehicle card. The most common ones:

- the Name is empty, or the Name, Version or Author contains `"`, `\` or a line break;
- the Price is 0;
- the internal name is too long: at most 12 characters, or 10 when a truck of the project has one main texture per cabin layout;
- a game version is badly written, or not supported by one of your vehicles;
- a vehicle's game data is missing, or two vehicles are the same vehicle in the game.

The same list also shows warnings that don't block the export: empty textures are exported transparent, so the game's base color shows; textures **To check** after a template update deserve a look. See [the Project space](../../fleet/project/).

## Export the mod

1. Click <Ui id="top-bar-export" /> in the top bar, from any space, or choose <Ui id="menu-file" /> › <Ui id="cmd-export-mod" /> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>E</kbd>).
2. Check the summary: each vehicle with the textures it paints, and the cabins left unpainted.
3. Check the <Ui id="mod-destination" />, and click <Ui id="mod-change-destination" /> to choose another file.
4. Click <Ui id="mod-export-start" />.

<Shot name="export/export-mod-dialog" alt="The Export Mod dialog with the summary of the mod, its destination with Change…, a warning about empty textures, and the Cancel and Export buttons" />

<Clip name="export/export-mod" label="Clicking Export… in the top bar, then Export in the Export Mod dialog" />

<Ui id="mod-export-start" /> stays disabled while a problem remains, and the problems are listed above the buttons. Their <Ui id="project-show" /> button closes the dialog and takes you to the field to fix. If the file already exists, TruckPaint asks before replacing it.

The export runs in the background with its progress; cancelling leaves no file behind and an earlier export untouched. It never changes your project.

## Where the mod goes

The dialog proposes "Name.scs" in the mod folder chosen for the game on the <Ui id="prefs-tab-games" /> tab of the preferences, when it exists. Otherwise it uses the game's mod folder, when that folder exists:

| | Euro Truck Simulator 2 | American Truck Simulator |
|---|---|---|
| Windows | `Documents\Euro Truck Simulator 2\mod` | `Documents\American Truck Simulator\mod` |
| macOS | `~/Library/Application Support/Euro Truck Simulator 2/mod` | `~/Library/Application Support/American Truck Simulator/mod` |
| Linux | `~/.local/share/Euro Truck Simulator 2/mod` | `~/.local/share/American Truck Simulator/mod` |

Otherwise it proposes the folder of your last export, or your Documents folder: move the file into the game's `mod` folder yourself.

## Enable the mod in the game

1. Start the game and open the **Mod Manager** from the profile screen.
2. Find your mod by its name and picture, enable it, and confirm.
3. Load your profile and buy the paint job, under its Name, for your truck or trailer.

The textures are written as DDS with mipmaps, at each texture's full size. Templates are never included.
