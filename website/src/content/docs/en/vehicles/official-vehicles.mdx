The official trucks and trailers belong to SCS Software, so TruckPaint does not ship them. You make them from your own installed game, on your computer, in the <Ui id="vehicles-tab-game" /> tab of the [Vehicle Library](../library/). They are for your own use: TruckPaint never uploads them. <Ui id="home-new-project" /> and <Ui id="cmd-add-vehicle" /> list only installed vehicles. Once a vehicle is made, creating projects and exporting mods need no game installation.

## Set the game folder

Open the preferences and click the <Ui id="prefs-tab-games" /> tab. Each game has its own card, with a game folder and a mod folder. At the right of the game's name, its state shows in words: its version once the folder is valid, <Ui id="prefs-folder-not-set" /> while it is empty, or a folder problem. Each folder is one field, and its buttons sit inside it:

- <Ui id="prefs-game-choose" />, at the right of the field, picks the folder by hand: use it for GOG, Epic or any other place. You can also type the path of the game folder.
- The cross, shown once a folder is set, forgets it (<Ui id="prefs-game-clear" />).
- <Ui id="prefs-game-detect" />, at the foot of the card while the game folder is empty, looks for the game in your Steam libraries (Windows, Linux including the Flatpak version of Steam, and macOS).

A game is one you play when its folder is set: <Ui id="home-new-project" /> starts on the first game that has vehicles installed, or on the only game whose folder is set. TruckPaint refuses the folder of the other game. The <Ui id="prefs-mod-folder-label" /> row shows where <Ui id="cmd-export-mod" /> saves mods for that game, the detected folder when you chose none. It has <Ui id="prefs-game-choose" />, and the cross once you chose one, which goes back to the detected folder.

TruckPaint checks the folder, shows the game's version when it is valid, and says why when it is not. On the first launch it looks for the games once by itself.

## Make the vehicles

1. Open <Ui id="menu-vehicle" /> › <Ui id="cmd-vehicle-library" /> and click <Ui id="vehicles-tab-game" />. If no folder is set yet, TruckPaint proposes the one it found.
2. The list shows the trucks and trailers of your game. Only the DLC you own appear. Each row says whether it is <Ui id="game-status-not-generated" />, generated (with its version) or <Ui id="game-status-updated" />.
3. Check the vehicles you want, or use <Ui id="game-select-all" />, <Ui id="game-select-none" /> and <Ui id="game-select-updates" />.
4. Click <Ui id="game-generate" args={{ count: 3 }} />. The vehicles that are not made yet are made, and the others are made again.

<Shot name="vehicles/my-game" alt="The My Game tab filtered on trucks: three trucks, two of them marked Game updated and checked, then a Workshop section with one truck from a mod, and the Generate (2) button" />

The vehicles are made one after another, and each one is installed as soon as it is ready. A progress bar counts them ("1 of 6"), the vehicle being made is named, and each row shows whether it is waiting, in progress, installed, up to date or failed. You can keep working in the app, and close the library: a message tells you when it is done. <Ui id="button-cancel" /> stops before the next vehicle and keeps those already installed. A vehicle that fails does not stop the others; the result lists it with the reason.

<Shot name="vehicles/my-game-progress" alt="A run in progress: the first vehicle installed, the second in progress, four waiting, and the bar at 1 of 6" />

## Trucks from the Steam Workshop

A truck mod you subscribed to on the Steam Workshop is read the same way. Under the official vehicles, the <Ui id="vehicles-tab-game" /> tab has a <Ui id="game-workshop" /> section that lists the trucks of your Workshop mods, with the name of their mod. Check them and click <Ui id="game-generate" args={{ count: 2 }} /> like the others: they are made in the same run, one after another, and installed in the Vehicle Library with an id that starts with `mod.ets2`.

TruckPaint finds the Workshop folder by itself: it is in the same Steam library as your game (`steamapps/workshop/content/227300`). For each mod it uses the package that fits your game's version, and only looks at mods of the truck category. A truck the game already has (a retexture or an interior mod for an official truck) is not listed, because it is not a new truck. The mod is read on top of your game, so a mod that reuses the game's models works too.

- A Workshop truck belongs to its author. Its package is made on your computer for your own use, and its license says so.
- If a mod cannot be read, the section names it and says why. The other mods are listed as usual.
- A mod that needs a DLC you do not own may fail with a message naming the DLC.
- Workshop trucks are made one at a time, and a large mod can take a few minutes. After the mod is updated by Steam, check its truck and generate it again.
- If your Workshop is in another Steam library than the game, or the game is not in a Steam library, the section says the Workshop could not be found.

## After a game update

When the game moves to a new series of versions (for example from 1.61 to 1.62), the vehicles you made show <Ui id="game-status-updated" />. Click <Ui id="game-select-updates" />, then <Ui id="game-generate" args={{ count: 3 }} />. The new package gets the game's version, and the old one stays installed beside it, so your projects keep working.

If a template did not change, projects that use the vehicle are not affected. If the game changed one, projects see "template updated": see [The Vehicle Library](../library/). A vehicle that did not change is reported as up to date, and nothing is installed.

A patch that keeps the same series (1.61.1 then 1.61.2) is not flagged. To refresh a vehicle after such a patch, check it and generate it again.

## What is made

Each truck package holds all the cabins and painted accessories of the truck. Truck and cabin names are the game's English names. Cabin templates are 4096 × 4096; accessory sizes come from the game's paint definitions.

A trailer package has one main texture for every painted body, chassis and part that the game does not paint from its own region, and accessory textures shared by the whole trailer. Parts that the game paints from the same region in every paint job share one texture, so you paint them once. Trailers have no variants. SCS's own trailers are named "SCS" followed by their game name, such as "SCS Box". Trailers whose paint jobs only set a plain colour, such as the SCS flatbed, have no livery to paint, so they are not listed.

Templates trace the model's paint layout; they do not prove that a livery looks right in the game, so check new work in game.

## Limits

- Only ETS2 vehicles can be made for now. You can set and check the American Truck Simulator folder, and the tab says that its vehicles are not supported yet.
- Only the vehicles of your installed game and DLC are listed.
- Of the mods, only the trucks of your Steam Workshop mods are read. Trailer mods and the files you put in the game's mod folder by hand are not.
- A Workshop truck can be harder to paint than a game truck. Some mods put a whole cabin on one shared texture, and several trucks of a mod can share the same textures, so a livery then applies to all of them in the game.

The packages carry the license "Personal modding only, not for sale. Game content belongs to SCS Software." (a Workshop truck's says that the content belongs to the mod's author). TruckPaint is not endorsed by SCS Software. Exporting a package keeps a reminder to check that you may share it.

## Install and paint

Made vehicles are installed in the Vehicle Library already.

1. In <Ui id="home-new-project" />, check the vehicle, choose cabins and accessories, then use <Ui id="new-project-create" />.
2. Paint and save the project, then follow [Mod export](../../export/mod/).

## Retired packages

The Carsmaniac catalog and template pack workflow have been removed. Generated packages keep `official.ets2.<vehicle>`, including `official.ets2.daf.2021`. Remove old test packages manually before generating the replacements. There is no migration of old placements. Installing the same id and version replaces that installed version; another version is kept alongside it. Existing project files are not automatically rewritten.
